Why My Valuation Is More Accurate Than Zillow

Zillow's Zestimate is a starting point, not a valuation. Their algorithm doesn't visit your home, doesn't know about your new kitchen, doesn't understand that your two-family home has a legal C of O while the one across the street doesn't, and doesn't adjust for the fact that Queens neighborhoods can change block by block.

My Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) is built from:

  • Recent closed sales: I pull every comparable sale within a half-mile radius from the last 6 months — not asking prices, actual sold prices. I adjust for differences in size, condition, lot size, and property type.
  • Active listings: What your competition looks like right now. Buyers will compare your home to these listings, so I show you what you're up against.
  • Pending sales: Homes that are under contract but haven't closed yet. These are the most current indicator of market direction.
  • Neighborhood-level knowledge: I know which blocks command premiums, which buildings have co-op board issues, which streets flood, and which two-family homes have legal rental units. This matters more than any algorithm.

The typical Zestimate error margin in Queens is 6% to 8%. On a $700,000 home, that's $42,000 to $56,000 off. My CMA is typically accurate within 2% to 3%.

What Affects Your Home's Value in Queens

Queens home values are driven by factors that generic valuation tools often miss:

  • Legal two-family status: A legal two-family home with a Certificate of Occupancy is worth 15% to 25% more than an identical single-family home because of the rental income potential.
  • Lot size and zoning: Homes on larger lots or in zones that allow expansion command premiums. R4 and R4-1 zoning in southeast Queens allows detached two-family homes.
  • Renovation quality: A renovated kitchen and bathrooms can add $30,000 to $80,000 depending on the quality. But poor renovations (cheap finishes, unpermitted work) can hurt your value.
  • Block-level differences: In Queens, values can change $50,000 or more from one block to the next based on tree-lined streets, proximity to commercial zones, or school district boundaries.
  • Co-op building financials: For co-ops, the building's financial health (reserve fund, underlying mortgage, maintenance trajectory) affects your unit's value as much as the unit itself.
