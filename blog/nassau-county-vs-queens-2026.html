The Big Picture — Queens vs. Nassau County in 2026

This is the most common crossroads I see: a family outgrowing their Queens apartment or co-op, ready to buy a house, trying to decide between staying in Queens or crossing the border into Nassau County. Both have real advantages. Neither is universally better. The right answer depends on your commute, your kids' ages, your budget, and what kind of neighborhood you want to live in.

I work in both markets — across all of Queens and western Nassau County including Elmont, Valley Stream, Franklin Square, and Floral Park. Here's the honest breakdown.

Property Taxes — The Biggest Difference

This is where the conversation starts for most families. The property tax difference between Queens and Nassau County is dramatic and affects your monthly housing cost more than most buyers realize.

Queens: NYC property taxes on residential homes are calculated using a complex assessment system, but the effective rate on a single-family home works out to roughly 0.9% of market value. On a $700,000 home, that's approximately $6,300 per year, or $525 per month.

Nassau County: The effective property tax rate averages 2.2% of market value — one of the highest in the United States. On the same $700,000 home, taxes are approximately $15,400 per year, or $1,283 per month. That's $757 more per month than Queens.

However, Queens residents pay New York City income tax (3.08% to 3.88% of taxable income), which Nassau County residents do not. For a household earning $150,000, the city income tax is approximately $5,000 to $5,800 per year. This offsets some — but not all — of the property tax difference.

Net cost comparison: For a typical family earning $150,000 buying a $700,000 home, the all-in tax burden is roughly similar between Queens and western Nassau County. But for higher-value homes ($1M+), Nassau County taxes become significantly more expensive.

School Districts

Schools are the primary reason families move from Queens to Nassau County. And the reputation is largely deserved — Nassau County has some of the best public school districts in New York State. But Queens has stronger options than most buyers realize.

Nassau County Standouts

  • Great Neck: Consistently ranked top 10 in NYS. Average SAT scores above 1300. Strong AP offerings. Homes start at $800,000.
  • Jericho: Top 5 in NYS. Exceptional STEM programs. Homes from $750,000.
  • Manhasset: Small, elite district. Per-pupil spending among the highest in the state. Homes from $900,000.
  • Garden City: Traditional suburban district with strong academics and athletics. Homes from $700,000.
  • Sewanhaka CHSD (Elmont area): Solid schools at more affordable price points. Homes from $500,000.

Queens Standouts

  • District 26 (Bayside, Fresh Meadows, Douglaston): The best public school district in NYC. Test scores rival many Nassau County districts. Homes from $650,000.
  • Specialized high schools: Queens is home to several NYC specialized high schools with admissions by exam.
  • Gifted and Talented programs: Available borough-wide with admissions testing.

Commute and Transportation

Queens wins on transit. The subway system connects most Queens neighborhoods directly to Manhattan — no transfers, no additional fares, no schedule dependence. Nassau County relies on the LIRR, which is efficient but expensive and runs on a schedule.

  • Western Queens (Astoria, LIC): 15 to 25 minutes to Midtown by subway. $2.90 per ride.
  • Central Queens (Forest Hills, Rego Park): 25 to 35 minutes by subway.
  • Southeast Queens (Jamaica, Ozone Park): 35 to 55 minutes by subway, or 20 minutes by LIRR from Jamaica Station.
  • Western Nassau (Elmont, Valley Stream): 30 to 45 minutes by LIRR to Penn Station. Monthly pass: $200 to $350.
  • Central Nassau (Garden City, Mineola): 35 to 50 minutes by LIRR.

If you work in Manhattan and don't want to own a car, Queens is the clear choice. If you work on Long Island or work from home, Nassau County's transit disadvantage doesn't matter.

Home Prices and Property Types

Surprisingly, median prices are similar between Queens and western Nassau County. But you get very different homes for the money.

Queens $700,000: A two-family brick row house in Ozone Park or Woodhaven on a 2,500 sq ft lot. Or a detached single-family in Jamaica. Or a large co-op in Forest Hills.

Nassau $700,000: A detached Cape Cod or colonial on a 6,000 sq ft lot in Valley Stream, Elmont, or Franklin Square. Private driveway, yard, possibly a garage. Newer construction than most Queens homes.

Key difference: Queens has two-family homes everywhere — your best path to mortgage offset through rental income. Nassau County zoning restricts two-family homes to a few areas. If rental income is part of your financial plan, Queens is the better choice.

Lifestyle and Culture

Queens is the most diverse county in the United States. Every cuisine, every culture, every language — it's all here. Walk down Roosevelt Avenue in Jackson Heights and you'll hear Spanish, Nepali, Bengali, Hindi, and Tagalog in a single block. Richmond Hill has the best Indo-Caribbean food in North America. Flushing has authentic Chinese and Korean food that rivals anything in Asia.

Nassau County is more homogeneous but not without diversity. Western Nassau (Elmont, Hempstead, Valley Stream) has significant Black, Caribbean, and South Asian populations. Hicksville has a thriving Indian community. But the overall feel is suburban — quiet streets, car-dependent shopping, and less walkable neighborhoods.

If you want cultural energy and walkability, Queens wins. If you want a quiet suburban street with a yard where your kids ride bikes, Nassau County wins.

Who Should Buy in Queens

  • Manhattan commuters who want subway access
  • Buyers who want a two-family home with rental income
  • Families who value cultural diversity and walkable neighborhoods
  • First-time buyers with tighter budgets (co-ops from $200,000)
  • Investors looking for rental property

Who Should Buy in Nassau County

  • Families prioritizing top-ranked school districts
  • Buyers who want larger lots and detached homes
  • Remote workers or Long Island commuters
  • Families who prefer a quieter, suburban environment
  • Buyers who don't need rental income from a second unit

Western Nassau County — The Border Towns

The most popular Nassau County options for Queens families are the border towns — close enough to maintain Queens connections while gaining suburban benefits:

  • Elmont: Directly adjacent to Queens. Strong South Asian and Caribbean communities. Homes from $500,000 to $850,000. Belmont Park and the new UBS Arena nearby.
  • Valley Stream: Multiple LIRR stations. Green Acres Mall for shopping. Diverse community. Homes from $500,000 to $900,000. Good school options.
  • Franklin Square: Quiet, family-oriented. Good schools (Sewanhaka district). Homes from $500,000 to $750,000. Close to Southern State Parkway.
  • Floral Park (Nassau): Charming village with walkable downtown. Excellent Sewanhaka or Floral Park-Bellerose school district. Homes from $550,000 to $950,000.

FAQ — Nassau County vs Queens

Are property taxes higher in Nassau County or Queens?

Nassau County property taxes are significantly higher — roughly 2.2% effective rate vs 0.9% in Queens. On a $700,000 home, that's $15,400/yr in Nassau vs $6,300/yr in Queens. However, Queens residents pay NYC income tax (3-4%), which Nassau residents don't, partially offsetting the difference.

Are schools better in Nassau County?

Nassau County generally ranks higher, with districts like Great Neck and Jericho among the state's best. But Queens' District 26 (Bayside, Fresh Meadows) rivals many Nassau districts. Queens also has NYC specialized high schools. The gap is narrower than many assume.

Is commuting easier from Queens or Nassau?

Queens has a major advantage with subway access — 20-55 minute rides to Midtown for $2.90. Nassau relies on LIRR at $200-$350/month for a pass. If you work in Manhattan and don't want a car, Queens is the clear winner.
