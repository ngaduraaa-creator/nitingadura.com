June 2026 Market Summary — Queens at a Glance

The Queens real estate market enters June 2026 in a position of cautious strength. After two years of mortgage rate volatility that froze inventory and sidelined buyers, the market is finding its footing. Rates have stabilized in the mid-6% range, inventory is slowly climbing, and transaction volume is recovering — though still below 2021-2022 peaks.

The headline: Queens remains a seller's market overall, but the intensity varies dramatically by neighborhood and property type. If you're buying or selling in Queens right now, the neighborhood-level data matters more than borough-wide averages.

Borough-Wide Median Sale Price: $685,000

Up 4.2% year-over-year. Single-family: $780,000. Two-family: $875,000. Co-ops: $285,000. Condos: $520,000.

Median Days on Market: 45 Days

Down from 52 days in June 2025. Well-priced listings in prime neighborhoods selling in 20-30 days. Overpriced listings sitting 90+ days.

Active Inventory: 3.2 Months Supply

Below the 6-month balanced market threshold. Inventory rising slowly from 2.8 months in March 2026, but still seller-favored borough-wide.

Mortgage Rates: 6.35%–6.65% (30-Year Fixed)

Stable compared to 2024-2025 volatility. FHA rates approximately 0.25% lower. 5/1 ARM starting at 5.75%.

What's Driving the Queens Market in June 2026

Interest Rate Stabilization

The biggest market driver in 2026 is rate stability. After bouncing between 6.1% and 7.4% throughout 2024 and early 2025, 30-year fixed rates have settled into a narrow 6.35% to 6.65% band. This stability is more important than the absolute number — buyers can plan, sellers can price, and deals can close without rate-lock anxiety.

For Queens specifically, rate stabilization has released pent-up demand from buyers who were waiting on the sidelines. Transaction volume in Q1 2026 was up 18% compared to Q1 2025, and that momentum is carrying into June.

Inventory is Rising — But Slowly

Active listings in Queens increased from 2.8 months in March 2026 to 3.2 months in June. This is healthy — more options for buyers without tipping into a buyer's market. The increase is concentrated in southeast Queens (Jamaica, St. Albans, Springfield Gardens) where homeowners who bought at 3% rates in 2020-2021 are finally listing.

Western Queens (Astoria, Long Island City, Sunnyside) remains inventory-starved, with under 2 months of supply. If you're selling in western Queens, you still have significant leverage.

Two-Family Homes Remain the Hottest Segment

Two-family homes in Queens are the strongest segment of the market. Buyers use rental income to qualify for larger mortgages, and the math works even at current rates. A $900,000 two-family home in Ozone Park with a rental unit generating $2,200/month effectively reduces the owner's net housing cost by $26,400 per year.

I'm seeing multiple offers on well-priced two-family listings in Richmond Hill, South Ozone Park, and Woodhaven. If you're selling a two-family home, this is an excellent time.

Co-op Market Recovering

The co-op market in Queens had the hardest recovery from the rate shock. Co-op buyers are typically first-time buyers with tighter budgets, and the jump from 3% to 6%+ rates priced many out. But as rates stabilize and co-op boards become more flexible with financing requirements, transaction volume is recovering.

Co-op median prices in Forest Hills ($310,000), Rego Park ($295,000), and Kew Gardens ($275,000) remain accessible compared to comparable Manhattan and Brooklyn apartments. For buyers priced out of those boroughs, Queens co-ops represent genuine value.

New Development Impact

Several new condo developments in Long Island City and Astoria are delivering units, adding inventory to the western Queens market. This is moderating price growth in those neighborhoods — condo prices in LIC are up only 2.1% year-over-year compared to 8%+ in 2021-2022. For buyers, this means more negotiating leverage on new development purchases.

Buyer Strategy — June 2026

If you're buying in Queens right now, here's my advice based on current market conditions:

  1. Get pre-approved before you start looking. In a seller's market with 3.2 months of inventory, sellers won't entertain offers without a pre-approval letter. I can connect you with lenders who process pre-approvals in 48 hours.
  2. Target the 30-to-60-day listings. Fresh listings get the most competition. Homes that have been on the market 30 to 60 days often have motivated sellers willing to negotiate, and the home may have been overpriced initially.
  3. Consider southeast Queens for value. Jamaica, St. Albans, and Springfield Gardens offer significantly lower price points with strong community infrastructure and improving transit access.
  4. Don't wait for rates to drop dramatically. The Fed has signaled modest cuts in late 2026, but rates are unlikely to return to 4-5% levels. Today's rates are the new normal. Buy now, refinance later if rates drop.
  5. Look at two-family homes. The rental income changes the math completely. Even at 6.5% rates, a two-family home with rental income can be more affordable monthly than a single-family home at a lower price point.

Seller Strategy — June 2026

If you're selling in Queens, here's how to maximize your outcome in the current market:

  1. Price accurately from day one. Overpricing in a stabilizing market leads to extended days on market and eventual price reductions that signal desperation. I use hyper-local comparable sales data — not Zillow estimates — to price every listing.
  2. Stage and photograph professionally. Buyers in 2026 are more discriminating than peak-frenzy 2021 buyers. Professional staging and photography are not optional — they're the minimum. Read my complete staging guide for Queens homes.
  3. Target the spring-summer window. June through September is historically the strongest selling season in Queens. Family buyers want to close before the school year. Don't wait until fall.
  4. Highlight rental income for two-family homes. Provide documentation of current rental income, lease terms, and market rent estimates. Buyers and their lenders want to see the numbers.

Looking Ahead — Q3 and Q4 2026 Forecast

Based on current data trends and economic indicators, here's what I expect for the Queens market through the rest of 2026:

  • Prices: Continued modest appreciation of 3% to 5% year-over-year. No crash, no boom. Neighborhood variation will be the story.
  • Inventory: Gradual increase toward 3.5 to 4 months by year end. Still seller-favored but less intensely so.
  • Rates: Likely stable in the 6.0% to 6.5% range with possible 25-basis-point cuts from the Fed in Q4 2026.
  • Transaction volume: Continued recovery, approaching 2019 levels by Q4 2026.

FAQ — Queens Real Estate Market June 2026

Is Queens a buyer's or seller's market in June 2026?

Queens remains a seller's market with 3.2 months of inventory — below the 6-month balanced threshold. However, conditions vary by neighborhood. Southeast Queens is closer to balanced at 4.5 months, while Astoria and Forest Hills are firmly seller-favored at under 2 months of inventory.

What is the median home price in Queens?

The borough-wide median sale price is approximately $685,000, up 4.2% year-over-year. Single-family homes median at $780,000, two-family homes at $875,000, co-ops at $285,000, and condos at $520,000. Prices vary significantly by neighborhood.

How long do homes take to sell in Queens?

The median days on market is 45 days. Well-priced homes in high-demand neighborhoods sell in 20 to 30 days. Two-family homes in southeast Queens average 50 to 65 days. Overpriced listings or homes needing significant work can sit for 90+ days.

What mortgage rates are available for Queens buyers?

30-year fixed rates average 6.35% to 6.65% depending on credit score and loan type. FHA rates run about 0.25% lower. 5/1 ARMs start around 5.75%. Rates have stabilized compared to 2024-2025, giving buyers more planning confidence.
