7 Costly Mistakes First-Time Home Buyers Make in Queens, NY

I'm Nitin Gadura, and I've guided hundreds of first-time buyers through the Queens market. These are the seven mistakes I see most often — each one can cost you thousands of dollars or the home you want. Here's how to avoid them.

Why First-Time Buying in Queens Is Different

Queens is not a generic suburban market. It has co-ops with board approvals, two-family homes with rental income calculations, row houses with party wall agreements, and condos with wildly varying HOA fees. The rules, costs, and pitfalls here are specific to this borough, and generic first-time buyer advice from national websites will leave you unprepared.

I work with first-time buyers in Ozone Park, Richmond Hill, Jamaica, Jackson Heights, Forest Hills, and across the borough every week. Here are the mistakes I see, and exactly how to avoid each one.

#1

House-Hunting Before Getting Pre-Approved

This is the most common and most damaging mistake. First-time buyers get excited, start browsing Zillow, book showings, and fall in love with a home they can't afford — or worse, make an offer that gets rejected because they have no pre-approval letter.

In Queens' competitive market, sellers and their agents won't even schedule a showing for a serious property without seeing your pre-approval. You're wasting your own time and the seller's time if you show up without one.

The real cost: You lose bidding wars to prepared buyers. You waste weeks looking at homes outside your price range. You get emotionally invested in properties you can't close on.

The fix: Get pre-approved before you look at a single home. I connect my buyers with lenders who process pre-approvals in 48 to 72 hours. This tells you your exact budget, your monthly payment, and what loan programs you qualify for. It also signals to sellers that you're serious.

#2

Not Understanding Co-op vs. Condo

Queens has more co-op apartments than any other property type. Forest Hills, Rego Park, Kew Gardens, Jackson Heights, and Flushing are dominated by co-ops. First-time buyers from out of state or from a rental background often don't understand the difference — and it matters enormously.

Co-op: You buy shares in a corporation. You don't own the apartment — you own stock in the co-op corporation that gives you the right to occupy a specific unit. There's a board interview. The board can reject you. There are restrictions on subletting, renovations, and even pets. Monthly maintenance includes property taxes. Financing is harder — not all lenders do co-op loans, and the building must be on the lender's approved list.

Condo: You own the physical unit with a deed. No board interview for purchase (though some condos have a right of first refusal). Easier to finance, sublet, and sell. But condos in Queens are generally 30% to 50% more expensive than comparable co-ops, and monthly common charges don't include property taxes.

The real cost: Buyers who don't understand co-op rules waste months applying to buildings where their financials don't meet the board's requirements, or they buy a co-op thinking they can rent it out and discover subletting is prohibited.

The fix: Before looking, decide whether co-op or condo fits your situation. I walk every first-time buyer through a comparison based on their income, savings, plans for subletting, and investment timeline. Read my co-op guide for a deep dive.

#3

Underestimating Closing Costs

First-time buyers budget for the down payment and forget about closing costs — which in Queens can add $15,000 to $35,000 on top of your down payment. This blindsides people at the worst possible moment.

Typical closing costs in Queens:

  • Mortgage origination fee: 0.5% to 1% of loan amount
  • Attorney fees: $1,500 to $3,000
  • Title insurance: $2,000 to $5,000 (houses/condos only; not applicable to co-ops)
  • Title search: $300 to $500
  • Recording fees: $200 to $500
  • Prepaid property taxes: 3 to 6 months
  • Homeowner's insurance prepayment: 1 year
  • Mortgage insurance (if under 20% down): varies
  • Co-op specific: flip tax (1% to 3%), move-in deposit ($500 to $1,000), application fee ($500 to $750)

The fix: Budget 3% to 5% of purchase price for closing costs, separate from your down payment. If you're buying a $600,000 property with 5% down, you need $30,000 for the down payment plus $18,000 to $30,000 for closing costs — total cash needed: $48,000 to $60,000. I provide a detailed closing cost estimate within 24 hours of any offer.

#4

Waiving the Home Inspection

During the 2021-2022 frenzy, some buyers waived inspections to win bidding wars. In 2026, the market has cooled enough that there's no excuse for this. Queens has some of the oldest housing stock in New York City — homes built in the 1920s through 1960s with potential issues that aren't visible during a walkthrough.

What inspections catch in Queens homes:

  • Foundation cracks and settling (especially in row houses)
  • Knob-and-tube wiring or aluminum wiring (fire hazard, insurance issue)
  • Lead paint (pre-1978 homes, which is most of Queens)
  • Asbestos in insulation, floor tiles, or pipe wrapping
  • Drainage and sewer line problems (tree roots in the lateral)
  • Illegal conversions — finished basements, attic apartments, or extra kitchens without permits
  • Roof damage hidden by recent cosmetic repairs

The real cost: A $45,000 foundation repair. A $15,000 electrical rewiring. A $20,000 sewer line replacement. These are real numbers from Queens homes I've seen.

The fix: Always get a home inspection. Budget $400 to $600 for a thorough inspection by a licensed professional. For older homes, add a sewer scope ($200 to $350) and a termite inspection ($75 to $150). I attend every inspection with my clients and know what to look for in Queens-specific construction.

#5

Ignoring Down Payment Assistance Programs

Many first-time buyers in Queens are eligible for programs that provide $10,000 to $100,000 in down payment assistance — and they never apply because they don't know these programs exist.

Available programs for Queens buyers:

  • SONYMA (State of New York Mortgage Agency): Below-market interest rates and up to $15,000 in down payment assistance for first-time buyers. Income limits apply.
  • NYC HomeFirst: Up to $100,000 in down payment and closing cost assistance for income-qualified NYC buyers. Forgivable over 10 to 15 years.
  • FHA loans: Only 3.5% down required (vs. 20% conventional). Gift funds from family allowed for the entire down payment.
  • Conventional 3% down loans: Some lenders offer 3% down conventional mortgages for first-time buyers with good credit.

The fix: Tell your agent and lender that you want to explore every available assistance program. I connect my first-time buyers with lenders who specialize in SONYMA and HomeFirst applications. These programs have income limits and property requirements — you need a knowledgeable lender, not just any mortgage broker.

#6

Shopping for the Home Before Shopping for the Loan

First-time buyers often go to one lender — usually their current bank — and accept whatever rate and terms they're offered. They don't realize that mortgage rates, fees, and loan programs vary significantly between lenders, and that the difference can be tens of thousands of dollars over the life of the loan.

The real cost: A 0.25% rate difference on a $500,000 mortgage saves approximately $26,000 over 30 years. A lender with $3,000 in higher origination fees costs you $3,000 at closing for the same loan. Not shopping around is literally throwing money away.

The fix: Get quotes from at least three lenders. I provide my buyers with a vetted list of lenders who work specifically in the Queens market and offer competitive rates. Include at least one credit union, one direct lender, and one mortgage broker. Compare not just rates but total closing costs, loan officer responsiveness, and processing speed.

#7

Buying the Home Without a Real Estate Attorney

New York State requires attorneys for real estate transactions, but some first-time buyers try to use the seller's attorney or a cheap online service. In Queens, where property issues are common and closing paperwork is complex, this is a dangerous shortcut.

What your attorney should catch:

  • Title defects — liens, judgments, unpaid taxes, boundary disputes
  • Illegal conversions or unpermitted work that could trigger DOB violations
  • Co-op proprietary lease terms that limit your rights
  • Contract clauses that favor the seller at your expense
  • Zoning or usage restrictions that affect your plans

The fix: Hire your own real estate attorney before you sign any contract. Budget $1,500 to $3,000. The attorney reviews the contract, conducts the title search, and represents you at closing. I refer my clients to attorneys who specialize in Queens real estate and know the local quirks — co-op boards, two-family legality, and NYC DOB regulations.

Bonus: The One Thing Most First-Time Buyers Get Right

Despite all these pitfalls, I see first-time buyers in Queens get one thing right consistently: they work with an agent who knows the borough. Generic agents who cover all five boroughs can't tell you whether a Richmond Hill two-family has a legal rental unit, or whether a Forest Hills co-op board will approve your financing, or whether a Jamaica fixer-upper is worth the renovation cost. Local knowledge is not optional in Queens.

Common Questions

FAQ — First-Time Home Buyers in Queens

What is the biggest mistake first-time buyers make in Queens?

House-hunting before getting mortgage pre-approval. Without pre-approval, you don't know your true budget, sellers won't take your offer seriously, and you risk falling in love with homes you can't afford. Get pre-approved first — I can connect you with lenders who process approvals in 48 to 72 hours.

What is the difference between a co-op and a condo in Queens?

In a co-op, you buy shares in a corporation — you don't own the apartment. Co-ops have board approval, subletting restrictions, and maintenance that includes taxes. In a condo, you own the unit with a deed, can sublet more freely, and finance more easily. Co-ops are cheaper but harder to buy and sell.

How much are closing costs for first-time buyers in Queens?

Expect 3% to 5% of the purchase price. On a $500,000 co-op, that's $15,000 to $25,000. On a $700,000 house, $21,000 to $35,000. Includes attorney fees, title insurance, recording fees, and prepaid taxes. Always budget closing costs separately from your down payment.

Should I waive the home inspection in Queens?

Never. Queens has old housing stock with potential foundation, wiring, plumbing, and conversion issues that aren't visible during a walkthrough. A $400 to $600 inspection can save you $50,000+ in unexpected repairs. If a seller insists on waiving inspection, consider it a red flag.
