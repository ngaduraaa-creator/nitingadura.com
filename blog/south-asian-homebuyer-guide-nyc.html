Why South Asian Buyers Need a Different Kind of Guide

Standard real estate advice is written for a nuclear family with two W-2 incomes, no extended family considerations, and no cultural priorities beyond school districts and commute times. That doesn't describe most South Asian families buying a home in New York City.

South Asian homebuyers — whether Indian, Punjabi, Bangladeshi, Pakistani, Sri Lankan, or Nepali — share priorities that mainstream agents often don't understand: multigenerational living arrangements, joint family income structures, proximity to cultural institutions, and a view of homeownership that's deeply tied to family identity and long-term wealth building.

I'm not writing this guide from a textbook. I'm Punjabi. My office is in Ozone Park. I've sat in kitchens in Richmond Hill and Jackson Heights explaining FHA programs in Hindi. I've helped families pool resources across three generations to buy a two-family home. This is what I do every day.

Best Neighborhoods for South Asian Families

Jackson Heights

The cultural heart of South Asian New York. 74th Street between Roosevelt and 37th Avenue is lined with Indian restaurants, sari shops, jewelry stores, and grocery markets. Strong Bangladeshi and Pakistani communities along Diversity Plaza. Co-op apartments are the primary housing stock — affordable entry points from $200,000 to $400,000. The 7, E, F, M, and R trains provide excellent transit. The Ganesh Temple is nearby in Flushing.

Richmond Hill

Large Indo-Caribbean (Guyanese-Indian, Trinidadian-Indian) community alongside Punjabi and Sikh families. Multiple gurdwaras serve the Sikh community. Two-family and three-family homes dominate the housing stock — ideal for multigenerational living. Prices range from $650,000 to $1.2 million for two-family properties. Strong community infrastructure with Indian grocery stores, clothing shops, and restaurants.

Ozone Park

Growing Indian and Punjabi population. More affordable than Richmond Hill with similar housing stock. Two-family homes from $550,000 to $900,000. Close to the A train and JFK Airport. Good selection of Indian restaurants and grocery stores. My office is located here, and I watch this neighborhood's South Asian community grow every year.

Floral Park / Bellerose

Eastern Queens neighborhoods at the Nassau County border with a significant Indian and South Asian population. More suburban feel with single-family detached homes, yards, and quieter streets. Strong school districts (District 26). Prices for single-family homes range from $650,000 to $1.1 million. Good for families who want Queens proximity with a suburban lifestyle.

Flushing

Home to the iconic Hindu Temple Society of North America (Ganesh Temple). Diverse Asian community with growing South Asian presence. Mix of co-ops, condos, and single-family homes. Excellent transit (7 train terminus, LIRR). Co-ops from $200,000. Single-family homes from $700,000. Major Indian grocery stores and restaurants serve the community.

Multigenerational Living — Finding the Right Property

For many South Asian families, buying a home means buying a home for the entire family — parents, adult children, and sometimes grandparents. This isn't an afterthought; it's the primary requirement. Here's how I approach multigenerational searches:

Legal Two-Family Homes

The most popular option for South Asian multigenerational families in Queens. A legal two-family home has two separate dwelling units — typically one on each floor — with separate kitchens, bathrooms, and entrances. Parents live on one floor, the younger family on the other. Or the second unit generates rental income that offsets the mortgage.

  • Key requirement: The property must have a Certificate of Occupancy authorizing two dwelling units. Without this, the second kitchen is illegal, the lender won't count rental income, and you face DOB violations.
  • Price range: $600,000 to $1.2 million in southeast Queens neighborhoods.
  • FHA advantage: FHA loans allow you to count 75% of projected rental income from the second unit toward your qualifying income.

Mother-Daughter Homes

A single-family home with a separate accessory dwelling unit — often a finished basement or attached apartment. These provide privacy while keeping the family under one roof. In Queens, many homes have been converted this way, but not all conversions are legal. I verify legality before every offer.

Large Single-Family Homes

For families who prefer to live together without physical separation. Look for homes with 4+ bedrooms, multiple bathrooms, and a large kitchen — the kitchen is the center of South Asian family life. Detached colonials in Bayside, Fresh Meadows, and Bellerose offer the space.

Joint Family Income — How Multiple Borrowers Work

South Asian families frequently pool resources to buy a home. Parents contribute savings, adult children contribute income, and sometimes siblings share the mortgage. Here's how this works from a lending perspective:

  • Co-borrowers: Multiple family members can appear on the mortgage application as co-borrowers. Each co-borrower's income is counted toward qualification. All co-borrowers must appear on the title.
  • Non-occupant co-borrowers: FHA allows a parent or family member who will NOT live in the home to co-sign. This is especially useful when parents have strong income but the home is for the younger generation. The non-occupant co-borrower must be a family member.
  • Gift funds: Family members can gift the entire down payment. The lender requires a signed gift letter stating the money is a gift, not a loan. No repayment expected.
  • Bank statement loans: For self-employed South Asian professionals — restaurant owners, IT consultants, small business operators — bank statement programs evaluate 12 to 24 months of deposits instead of tax returns. Particularly useful when tax returns show lower income due to business deductions.

I work with lenders who regularly process applications from South Asian families with multiple borrowers, gift funds, and self-employment income. The lender you choose matters enormously — a lender unfamiliar with these structures will slow your process or deny your application unnecessarily.

Cultural Timing — When to Buy

Many South Asian families time major purchases around auspicious dates, festivals, and family events. I respect this completely. Here's how cultural timing intersects with the real estate market:

  • Diwali season (October-November): Many families prefer to close on a new home around Diwali — beginning a new chapter in a new home during the festival of new beginnings. I plan backward from the desired closing date to ensure the search, offer, and mortgage process align.
  • Spring Navratri (March-April): Another popular time for new beginnings. The spring market also coincides with peak real estate inventory in Queens.
  • Avoid Shradh (Pitru Paksha): Some Hindu families prefer not to close or make major financial commitments during Shradh. I respect this and plan accordingly.
  • Vastu considerations: Some buyers want Vastu-compliant homes — northeast-facing entrance, kitchen in the southeast, master bedroom in the southwest. While perfect Vastu compliance is rare in Queens housing stock, I can filter for homes that meet the most important directional preferences.

Financing Programs for South Asian Buyers

FHA Loans

The most popular option for first-time South Asian buyers. 3.5% down, gift funds allowed, rental income counted for two-family homes. Available to green card holders, H-1B visa holders with valid work authorization, and citizens.

SONYMA

New York State's first-time homebuyer program with below-market rates and up to $15,000 in down payment assistance. Income limits apply but many Queens-based South Asian families qualify. A homebuyer education course is required — available online in English and sometimes Hindi.

Conventional Loans

For buyers with 10% to 20% down and strong credit. Lower mortgage insurance costs than FHA. Some lenders offer 3% down conventional loans for first-time buyers. Better option for buyers with high credit scores (740+) and significant savings.

H-1B Visa Buyer Programs

Specific lenders offer mortgage programs for H-1B visa holders without requiring a green card. Requirements include a valid H-1B, a minimum of one year of US credit history, and standard income documentation. I maintain a list of lenders who actively serve H-1B buyers in the NYC area.

Self-Employment and South Asian Buyers

A significant portion of South Asian homebuyers in Queens are self-employed — running restaurants, convenience stores, gas stations, taxi medallions, IT consulting businesses, or medical practices. Self-employment creates specific mortgage challenges:

  • Tax returns may show lower income due to legitimate business deductions, even though actual cash flow is strong.
  • Multiple business entities can complicate income documentation.
  • Cash-intensive businesses (restaurants, retail) require additional documentation.

I work with lenders who offer bank statement loan programs — they evaluate 12 to 24 months of business bank deposits instead of tax returns. This is often the right path for self-employed South Asian buyers whose tax returns understate their true earning capacity.

Community Infrastructure I Help You Find

When I search for homes for South Asian families, I don't just look at the property — I look at the community around it. Here's what I map for every buyer:

  • Temples and gurdwaras: Proximity to the Ganesh Temple in Flushing, gurdwaras in Richmond Hill, and other places of worship.
  • Indian grocery stores: Patel Brothers, Apna Bazar, Subzi Mandi, and local Indian grocery stores within reasonable distance.
  • South Asian restaurants: Desi restaurants, sweet shops, and bakeries that serve the community.
  • Cultural organizations: Community centers, language schools, and cultural associations.
  • School quality: Districts 26 (eastern Queens), 28 (central-south Queens), and 30 (western Queens) and their specific school performance data.
  • Transit access: Commute times to Manhattan, Midtown, and major employment centers.

Long Island — When Queens Isn't Big Enough

Many South Asian families who start their search in Queens end up looking at western Nassau County for more space, larger lots, and strong school districts. Here's the comparison:

  • Elmont: Right across the Queens border. Strong South Asian community. Single-family homes from $550,000 to $850,000. Elmont School District or Sewanhaka Central High School District.
  • Valley Stream: Suburban feel with good train access to Penn Station. Growing Indian population. Homes from $500,000 to $900,000.
  • Hicksville: One of Long Island's largest Indian communities. Strong South Asian shopping on Broadway. Homes from $550,000 to $1 million. Hicksville LIRR station provides a 45-minute commute to Penn Station.

FAQ — South Asian Home Buyers in NYC

Where do most South Asian families buy homes in Queens?

Jackson Heights has the largest concentration of Indian, Bangladeshi, and Pakistani communities. Richmond Hill and Ozone Park have strong Indo-Caribbean and Punjabi populations. Floral Park and Bellerose offer a more suburban feel with excellent schools. On Long Island, Hicksville, Elmont, and Valley Stream are popular.

Can joint family income be used for a mortgage?

Yes. Multiple family members can be co-borrowers to combine incomes. Parents can be non-occupant co-signers on FHA loans. Gift funds from family members are allowed for the entire down payment. I work with lenders who understand South Asian family purchasing structures.

What multigenerational home options exist in Queens?

Legal two-family homes are the most common option — separate units on different floors with their own entrances. Mother-daughter homes offer a separate accessory unit. Large single-family homes with 4+ bedrooms work for families who prefer to live together. Richmond Hill, South Ozone Park, and Ozone Park have the best inventory.
