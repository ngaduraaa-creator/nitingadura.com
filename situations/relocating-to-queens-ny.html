Why People Choose Queens

Queens gets overlooked by people who equate New York City with Manhattan. That's a mistake. Queens offers what no other borough can match: genuine diversity, real housing affordability (relative to Manhattan and Brooklyn), excellent transit to Midtown, and neighborhoods that range from urban excitement to genuine suburban quiet — all within one borough.

If you're relocating from another state, here's why Queens deserves your serious consideration:

  • Space and value: For the price of a one-bedroom condo in Manhattan, you can buy a two-family home in Queens with rental income that offsets your mortgage.
  • Cultural community: Whatever community you're part of, it exists in Queens. Indian, Chinese, Korean, Colombian, Filipino, Guyanese, Bangladeshi, Greek, Italian — every culture has a neighborhood here.
  • Schools: District 26 in eastern Queens is the highest-performing school district in New York City, rivaling many suburban districts.
  • Transit: Most Queens neighborhoods are on the subway, giving you a 20-to-55 minute commute to Midtown Manhattan without a car.
  • Airports: JFK and LaGuardia are both in Queens. If you travel frequently for work, this matters.

Neighborhoods by Lifestyle

Queens has 60+ distinct neighborhoods. Here's how I categorize them for families relocating from out of state:

Urban and Walkable

Astoria

Young, vibrant, walkable. Greek heritage mixed with new restaurants and bars. N/W subway to Midtown in 15 minutes. Studios from $1,800/month rent, co-ops from $350,000, condos from $500,000. Best for: young professionals, couples, people who want Manhattan energy at Queens prices.

Long Island City

Waterfront high-rises with Manhattan skyline views. Newest housing stock in Queens — modern condos with amenities. 7 train to Grand Central in 10 minutes. Studios from $2,500/month rent, condos from $600,000. Best for: corporate relocations, dual-income couples, people who want new construction.

Family-Friendly Suburban Feel

Bayside

Tree-lined streets, detached homes with yards, and District 26 schools — the best public schools in NYC. LIRR access plus Q27/Q31 buses. Single-family homes from $700,000. Best for: families with school-age children who want suburban living with NYC access.

Forest Hills

Charming village with Austin Street shopping district, Forest Hills Gardens, and excellent co-op inventory. E/F/M/R subway. Co-ops from $250,000, houses from $800,000. Best for: families and professionals who want walkable charm with good transit.

Howard Beach

Waterfront community near JFK with a distinctly suburban feel. Detached homes with garages and yards. A train access. Houses from $600,000. Best for: families who want quiet, space, and proximity to the airport.

Cultural Community Hubs

Jackson Heights

The most diverse neighborhood in the world. Massive South Asian, Latin American, and Filipino communities. 74th Street is Little India. Roosevelt Avenue is Little Colombia. Co-ops from $200,000. Best for: South Asian families, international transplants, people who want to live in their cultural community.

Flushing

The largest Chinatown outside of Asia. Also home to Korean, Indian, and South Asian communities. 7 train terminus. Co-ops from $200,000, houses from $700,000. Best for: Asian families, people who value authentic international cuisine and grocery access.

Richmond Hill

Little Guyana and a major Punjabi/Sikh community. Liberty Avenue is the cultural heart. Two-family homes from $650,000. Best for: Indo-Caribbean families, Punjabi and Sikh families, multigenerational buyers.

Affordable Entry Points

Jamaica

Major transit hub (LIRR, E/J/Z trains, AirTrain to JFK). Undergoing redevelopment with new retail and housing. Two-family homes from $500,000. Best for: first-time buyers, commuters, investors looking for appreciation potential.

Ozone Park

Diverse working-class neighborhood with strong community feel. A train access. Two-family homes from $550,000. Growing Indian, Guyanese, and Caribbean populations. My office is here. Best for: budget-conscious families, multigenerational buyers, first-time buyers.

Transit — Getting Around Queens

Queens has excellent transit, but it's organized in a radial pattern — most subway lines run between Queens and Manhattan, not between Queens neighborhoods. Here's the transit picture:

  • 7 train: Runs from Times Square through Sunnyside, Woodside, Jackson Heights, Corona, and Flushing. The backbone of western Queens.
  • E/F/M/R trains: Serve central Queens — Forest Hills, Rego Park, Kew Gardens, Jamaica.
  • J/Z trains: Serve Woodhaven, Richmond Hill, and connect to downtown Manhattan.
  • A train: The express to Midtown from Howard Beach, Ozone Park, and the Rockaways.
  • LIRR: Commuter rail from Jamaica, Woodside, and other stations. Faster than subway for some routes.
  • Buses: Extensive bus network fills gaps between subway lines. Q buses run local, QM express buses go to Manhattan.

A car in Queens: Unlike Manhattan, many Queens residents own cars. Street parking is available in most neighborhoods (some require permits). Having a car is helpful in eastern and southern Queens but not necessary in western Queens.

Schools in Queens

Queens has 32 community school districts. Performance varies enormously. Here's the essential breakdown for relocating families:

  • District 26 (Bayside, Fresh Meadows, Douglaston): Top-rated in NYC. Elementary schools consistently score in the top 5% citywide. If schools are your top priority and you want public school, this is where to look.
  • District 25 (Flushing, Whitestone): Strong performer with diverse student body. Good elementary and middle school options.
  • District 28 (Forest Hills, Rego Park, Kew Gardens): Solid schools in walkable neighborhoods. Good co-op inventory nearby.
  • District 30 (Astoria, LIC, Sunnyside): Mixed performance but improving rapidly with neighborhood development.
  • Specialized high schools: Queens is home to several of NYC's specialized high schools (admissions by exam). The SHSAT exam is open to all NYC students.

Cost of Living — What to Expect

If you're coming from a lower cost-of-living area, Queens will feel expensive. If you're coming from Manhattan, it will feel like a bargain. Here's the realistic picture:

  • Housing (buy): Median price $685,000. Co-ops from $200,000. Two-family homes from $500,000. Single-family from $600,000.
  • Housing (rent): Studio $1,500–$2,000. One-bedroom $1,800–$2,500. Two-bedroom $2,200–$3,500. Varies widely by neighborhood.
  • Groceries: Comparable to national average at chain stores. Ethnic grocery stores (Indian, Chinese, Caribbean) often have lower prices than mainstream supermarkets.
  • Transportation: MetroCard unlimited monthly: $132. LIRR monthly: $200–$350. Car insurance: $2,000–$4,000/year (higher than national average).
  • Taxes: NYC income tax (3%–4%), NYS income tax (4%–9%), property tax (~0.9% effective). No sales tax on clothing under $110.

Renting vs. Buying When You Relocate

My recommendation for most relocating families: rent for 6 to 12 months first, then buy. Here's why:

  • You need to understand the neighborhoods firsthand before committing to a purchase.
  • Your commute pattern will become clear only after you've lived it.
  • Building credit history and employment history in NYC strengthens your mortgage application.
  • You'll have time to save for a down payment while paying rent that's lower than Manhattan or Brooklyn.

FAQ — Relocating to Queens

What is the cost of living in Queens?

Queens is more affordable than Manhattan and Brooklyn. Median home price is $685,000. One-bedroom rent ranges $1,800-$2,500. Groceries are comparable to national average at chain stores, often cheaper at ethnic markets. Property taxes are lower than suburban Long Island. NYC income tax of 3-4% is an additional cost.

What are the best neighborhoods for families?

Bayside and Fresh Meadows for top-rated schools (District 26). Forest Hills for walkable charm and good schools. Howard Beach for space and yards. For South Asian families, Jackson Heights and Richmond Hill offer strong cultural communities. Budget-conscious families should look at Jamaica and Ozone Park.

How does the subway work in Queens?

Multiple subway lines serve Queens: 7 (western Queens), E/F/M/R (central), J/Z (south-central), A (southeast). Single ride $2.90, unlimited monthly $132. Runs 24/7. Most neighborhoods are 30-55 minutes from Midtown Manhattan. LIRR commuter rail provides faster service from Jamaica and other stations.
